## The page starts from what she already wrote  `00:00:00.000`

- Marion is seventy-one and has been planning her own funeral for months.
- Her memorial page is the part of that plan strangers will actually read.
- Start from my plan — her story, her photos and her song come across as they are.
- The page exists, and it already knows who it is about.

## A design that looks like a person, not a form  `00:00:22.952`

- Dozens of designs, and the one she picks is the one her family will recognise.
- Her name and her dates are already here, exactly as her plan holds them.
- The headline is the one thing only she could write.
- From here the page follows the plan — she edits once, in one place.

## Everything she wrote is already on the page  `00:00:44.433`

- The life story she wrote in her plan is here, as text she can still rewrite.
- The photo she filed under Memorial page is already on it, and still hers in the plan.
- And the song she chose for this page, not the one meant for the service.

## One link, and no account to open it  `00:01:03.004`

- Published — and from here it is a link, not an app anyone has to install.
- This is what her family opens, her photograph over the design she chose.
- And her song, on the page she built while she still could.

## A neighbour writes to her, with no account  `00:01:22.006`

- Tom lived across the street for thirty years. He was sent the link.
- His message is taken, and the page does not move. Nothing posts itself here.

## Nothing reaches her page until she accepts it  `00:01:40.363`

- Back in Marion's plan, where Tom's message is waiting for her.
- Every tribute waits in a queue, whoever wrote it and however kind it is.
- She decides, one by one, what her page is going to carry.
- Accepted, and only now is Tom's message part of the page.
